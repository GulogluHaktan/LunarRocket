Figure 2 and Figure 3 are images captured directly from simulation, from running a trained policy in the real Isaac Lab/Isaac Sim environment (offscreen render, via a checkpoint loaded with ISAACLAB_CHECKPOINT; captured in the final phase of the landing approach, with the vehicle roughly 0.6-0.7 m above the ground). Two changes were made for this capture: (i) the scene's default shadowless dome light was dimmed and a low-angle directional "sun" light casting real shadows was added, so that terrain relief and shadows become visible; (ii) the landing area was rendered with the 512x512-resolution real high-detail local patch (see Section 3.1, "local regolith pattern") in addition to the 128x128 global surface, so that centimetre-scale fine texture (roughness at the dust/sand-grain level) also becomes visible. That the vehicle is genuinely upright is based not on an assumption but on the simulation's own body-orientation data: throughout this run, the alignment of the body's local up-axis with the world +z axis (up_z) was measured constant at +1.000, i.e. the vehicle is not tipped over but fully vertical. Figure 2 shows the four-legged lander on the real cratered/rocky lunar surface; the ridge line, the grainy/rough surface texture, the shadows, and the scattered rocks are the DEM-based macro surface and procedural rock/crater/fine-detail fields defined in Section 3.1 in working condition. Figure 3 is a close-up of the lander in the same frame; the body, the tips of the two-axis gimbal thrust axis, and the grainy texture of the ground are visible together. These images are presented only to document the environment visually; no claim is made that they represent a particular landing outcome (successful/unsuccessful).
