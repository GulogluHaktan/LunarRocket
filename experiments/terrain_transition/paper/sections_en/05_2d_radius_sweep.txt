In the radius sweep performed to test whether this defect is independent of the transition radius (R = 4, 6, 8, 12, 16 m; Figure 9), the as-shipped Gaussian's C0 (RMS) error stays almost constant (between roughly 1.68e-3 and 1.76e-3 m), i.e. the error does not shrink as the transition radius grows; by contrast, the error of smoothstep and the calibrated Gaussian decreases steadily as the radius grows (as the curvature decreases), as expected. This shows that the as-shipped Gaussian's defect is not a discretization error but a scale-invariant structural property, independent of the transition radius.
