The analytic evaluation was carried out, with no Isaac Lab/torch dependency, on a one-to-one NumPy port of the formulas in the production code, along two independent tracks: (i) a mesh track, which generates the actual triangle mesh and measures vertex count and time; (ii) an analytic track, which queries the height field directly as a continuous function. Having the two tracks validate each other was used to show that the findings are not an artefact specific to mesh discretization.

The continuity metrics were computed with finite differences: C0 (height) continuity is defined as the absolute value of the height difference between points immediately on either side of the transition boundary (spatial epsilon = 0.05 m); C1 (slope) continuity as the angular difference of the local gradient at the same points (gradient epsilon = 0.02 m). Measurements were taken over 720 angular samples, and both the maximum and the RMS (root mean square) values are reported. In the initial measurements, the measurement made on the combined (global + local) height was seen to mix with the variation of the natural terrain slope over the epsilon interval and to suppress the actual seam signal; for this reason a "detail-only" measurement mode was defined, isolating only the faded fine-detail component (fade(r) * fine_detail(x,y)), and this was adopted as the study's headline metric; the measurement on the combined surface was retained as a secondary/realistic reference.

Memory cost was estimated separately for the two-mesh (literal, separate global and local meshes) and single-grid (baked, one combined mesh) accounting schemes, under the assumption of 32 bytes per vertex and a 2x multiplier for the collision mesh. Query throughput was also measured: the cost in microseconds of the pure fade formula (fade-only, isolated from terrain sampling), under the assumption of 512 environments and 64 global + 24 local + 4 fine-detail queries per environment (92 queries/environment in total).

The main comparison was repeated over thirty independent random seeds for the four method variants (smoothstep, as-shipped Gaussian, calibrated Gaussian, hybrid); statistical significance was assessed with the Wilcoxon signed-rank test on the per-seed log10 ratio differences (this test was preferred over parametric tests because in some comparisons -- e.g. hybrid versus smoothstep, which are analytically identical at r = R -- the difference distribution converges to near-zero variance and parametric effect-size measures such as Cohen's d degenerate).

The robustness of the findings was tested with four separate ablation analyses: (i) a contact-radius (rc) sweep over 1.0-4.0 m; (ii) a switch-width (w) sweep over 0.05-2.0 m; (iii) a transition-radius (R) sweep over 4-16 m, to test the scale-invariance of the defect; (iv) a grid-resolution sweep over roughly 0.31-1.27 m grid spacing, estimating the empirical convergence order by log-log linear regression.
