All nine training runs (3 methods x 3 seeds) completed without error, each reaching 5,734,400 environment steps (Table 5). The wall-clock time, as a method average, is 2377 +- 98 seconds for smoothstep, 2850 +- 898 seconds for the calibrated Gaussian, and 2674 +- 599 seconds for the hybrid. These averages show that the large difference observed in the first (seed=42) runs (Gaussian and hybrid 56% and 35% slower respectively) shrank substantially with multiple seeds: the three seed=7 and seed=123 runs all took similar times (2318-2345 seconds), while only the Gaussian and hybrid runs at seed=42 are markedly slower. The magnitude of the standard deviations (898 and 599 seconds, 20-30% of the mean) is the source of this single-run deviation; the first measurement's "35-56% slower" finding may therefore stem not from the blending formula itself but from a system-level variability specific to that particular run (background load, thermal state, or terrain-pool timing); averaged over the three seeds, the Gaussian and hybrid still appear slower than smoothstep (+20% and +12.5%), but this difference is no longer as sharp as in the first measurement.
