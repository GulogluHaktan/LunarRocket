This study systematically examined the shape of the blending function used at the boundary where a coarse global terrain and a fine-detail local patch meet, in an Isaac Lab-based lunar landing environment, both at the analytic/CPU level and in a real Isaac Sim SAC training run. Analytic measurements over many seeds showed, with statistical significance, that the previously used as-shipped Gaussian blend produces boundary continuity worse than smoothstep by several orders of magnitude when truncated at a finite radius, and that this defect is a scale-invariant, structural property. The proposed hybrid method and a calibrated Gaussian variant both removed this defect to near machine precision; this is the study's most robust and least contentious finding.

The real training comparison began with a single seed per method and, in that first measurement, showed the Gaussian and hybrid reaching a markedly higher success rate than smoothstep. Extending the study with additional independent seeds found no statistically significant difference among the methods on any metric, which turns the first measurement into a concrete, self-sourced example of how misleading single-run RL comparisons can be. Taken together with the analytic results, the choice of blending function has a measurable and robust effect on boundary continuity, but at this scale that effect is not distinguishable in final training performance. All three methods are integrated into the production code as a backward-compatible, selectable configuration, so the choice can rest on other criteria such as boundary continuity, which favours the Gaussian and hybrid, or computational cost, which favours smoothstep.

Three concrete directions are proposed for future work. First, the real training comparison should be extended with substantially more independent seeds, to determine whether the small mean differences observed, in particular the Gaussian consistently having the highest mean, are a real effect or residual noise. Second, the training scale should be raised to the full configuration, to confirm whether the observed plateaus reflect a final rather than an interim performance difference. Third, the external validity of the findings should be tested by repeating the same three blending methods on another physics engine, another reward and observation design, or another contact-rich task such as quadruped locomotion or humanoid standing.
