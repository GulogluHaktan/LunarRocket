This result does not confirm the finding, observed in the first (single-seed) measurement and discussed in detail in Section 5, that "the Gaussian and hybrid are significantly more successful than smoothstep." Going from n=1 to n=3, the effect size shrank (the Gaussian-smoothstep difference dropped from 11% to 4.7%) and the direction partly changed (the hybrid-smoothstep difference changed sign: in the first measurement the hybrid was higher than smoothstep, while in the three-seed average the hybrid is almost equal to smoothstep, even slightly lower). This pattern shows that the first finding is -- at least largely -- consistent with random run-to-run variance; this point is discussed in detail in Section 5. It is also the behaviour the reinforcement learning methodology literature predicts: Colas et al. (2018) show that the seed counts typically reported are far below the statistical power needed to resolve differences of this size, and Agarwal et al. (2021) demonstrate that point estimates computed from a handful of runs routinely reverse once uncertainty is reported properly.
