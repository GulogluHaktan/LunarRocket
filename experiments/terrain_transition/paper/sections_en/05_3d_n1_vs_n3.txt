This result does not confirm the finding, observed in the first (single-seed) measurement and discussed in detail in Section 5, that "the Gaussian and hybrid are significantly more successful than smoothstep." Going from n=1 to n=3, the effect size shrank (the Gaussian-smoothstep difference dropped from 11% to 4.7%) and the direction partly changed (the hybrid-smoothstep difference changed sign: in the first measurement the hybrid was higher than smoothstep, while in the three-seed average the hybrid is almost equal to smoothstep, even slightly lower). This pattern shows that the first finding is -- at least largely -- consistent with random run-to-run variance; this point is discussed in detail in Section 5.
